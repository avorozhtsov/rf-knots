## Your question restated

> Should we play with unknotting number, or start with "real math" restricted to a family like
> `Σ_{n≥1} p(n)/q(n)` for polynomials `p`, `q`?

And underneath it, the harder one:

> In math there are many complicated problems, but only some are important. How do we set
> direction for the agents?

## The selection criteria

A domain is a good *training environment* iff:

1. **exact, cheap verifier** — no LLM judges, no majority vote;
2. **planted solutions** — instances generated by inverting moves carry ground truth for free at
   every difficulty (this is what kills R-Zero's pseudo-label rot);
3. **smooth difficulty dial** — one integer the judge can turn;
4. **generation/solution asymmetry that is *bounded*** — generation must cost something, or the
   proposer game is degenerate;
5. **vectorizable** — fixed-shape state, jit-able transition, so self-play is cheap;
6. **an open frontier** — the ceiling is not a known algorithm.

## Scoring the three candidates

| | verifier | planted solutions | difficulty dial | bounded asymmetry | JAX-able | open frontier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Σ p(n)/q(n)` | ✅ perfect & **complete** | ✅ | ✅ (degrees, root type) | ✅ | ✅ (symbolic — no) | ❌ |
| unknotting / diagram simplification | ✅ one-sided (upper bounds certifiable) | ✅ excellent | ✅ (`K` scramble moves) | ✅ via budget | ✅ | ✅ |
| LMFDB / L-functions | ❌ (no move tree, no checker) | ❌ | ~ (conductor range) | n/a | ❌ | ✅✅ |

### On `Σ p(n)/q(n)`

This family is **completely solved by a known algorithm**. Partial fractions reduce it to values
of the digamma function `ψ` at rational points; convergence is `deg q ≥ deg p + 2` with no poles
at positive integers; rational roots give closed forms in `log 2`, `π`, `π cot(πa)`, harmonic
numbers. For the telescoping-summand version, **Gosper's algorithm decides** whether a
hypergeometric term has a hypergeometric antidifference, and **Zeilberger's algorithm** handles
definite sums. There is no frontier: the agents would be learning to emulate `sympy.summation`.

That is not worthless — it is an *excellent scaffolding harness*:
* the verifier is 10 lines of sympy;
* the difficulty dial is real (degree, irreducible factors, root field, whether Gosper succeeds);
* it runs on CPU in milliseconds, so you can debug the entire 5-agent league — scoring rule, IRT
  fitting, collapse detection, novelty dedup — for the price of coffee.

**Recommendation: build the league machinery on rational sums, in week 1–2, as a unit test.
Do not make it the science target.** If your league can't drive `θ` upward on a family where the
ground truth is a one-liner, it will not work anywhere.

### On unknotting

Everything the rational sums lack: an NP-hard search problem at the diagram level, an open
frontier (unknotting numbers are still unknown for many knots ≥11 crossings; papers in 2025–2026
are still improving bounds), and — crucially — a *proposer objective that is itself an open
question*: constructing hard unknot diagrams. It is also the one domain where the AlphaZero
machinery you already have in `../pgx-mcts-bench` applies literally.

**Recommendation: this is the science target.**

### On LMFDB / L-functions

Be clear-eyed: this is **a different kind of task**. Murmurations were found by *supervised
learning + human noticing*, not by a proposer/solver game. There is no move tree, no verifier,
no "solve" action. Scaling the knot game does not get you here.

What *could* transfer, later:
* the **difficulty/discrimination model** `d_φ` — "which instances are informative" is the same
  question in both domains;
* the **population as an ensemble of hypothesis generators** — 5 agents proposing *conjectures*
  about LMFDB statistics, scored by (a) survives numerical testing on held-out conductor ranges,
  (b) not implied by an already-known statement, (c) other agents fail to refute. That is a real
  game and it is the honest bridge, but it needs a *refutation* oracle rather than a solution
  verifier. Phase 3, not phase 1.

## The direction problem — my actual view

You are right that "interesting" cannot be discovered from inside a closed formal system. Goldbach
vs. Riemann is exactly the distinction: RH is load-bearing (hundreds of theorems are conditional
on it), Goldbach is a leaf. Load-bearing-ness is a *social/structural* property of the
mathematical corpus, not a property of the statement.

Three practical proxies, in increasing order of ambition:

1. **Import the direction.** Fix the family; let a human (or the literature) choose it. This is
   what you proposed and it is correct for phase 1. Direction = "improve unknotting number bounds
   on the standard tables", which the community already agrees is worth doing.
2. **Transfer as interestingness.** A problem is interesting iff training on it improves
   performance on a *held-out, externally chosen* benchmark. This is measurable, and it converts
   "interesting" into "informative for a fixed target distribution". It is the honest version of
   what R-Zero's 50% heuristic gestures at.
3. **Reuse / compression as interestingness.** A problem is interesting iff its solution contains
   a *lemma that gets reused* on other problems. Operationally: mine solution traces for recurring
   sub-sequences; a problem whose solution introduces a sub-sequence later reused across many other
   problems scores high. This is the closest computable analogue of "arises naturally from existing
   theory", and it is the one genuinely novel research direction in this project.
   In the knot env it is concrete: recurring move macros (e.g. a particular
   destabilize-commute-braid pattern) *are* lemmas.

I would build (1), instrument for (2), and treat (3) as the thesis-level contribution.

## Verdict

* **Phase 0 (weeks):** rational sums — harness, verifier, league, IRT, collapse detection. CPU only.
* **Phase 1 (months):** braid unknotting in pgx — Scrambler vs Simplifier, AlphaZero, one GPU.
  This is where the first real result lives.
* **Phase 2:** promote to the 5-agent LLM league on the knot family (LoRA population, PopuLoRA-style).
* **Phase 3:** conjecture-generation game on LMFDB data, with refutation instead of solution as the
  verifier.

Don't skip phase 0. Don't start phase 3 before phase 1 has produced a number a knot theorist cares about.
